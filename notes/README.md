# notes/

A lab notebook, not a final record. Entries here are dated and never
rewritten in place — corrections, doubts, and reinterpretations get
added alongside, not substituted in. Nothing gets deleted for being
wrong; a closed-off branch today may be exactly the opening someone
needs tomorrow, once combined with an idea we don't have yet.

Two different kinds of claims show up in these notes, and they deserve
different amounts of confidence:

- **Interpretation, connection, meaning.** Whether a piece of geometry
  says anything about prediction, physics, or "deep structure" — this is
  reading, not computation. It reflects the editors' current
  understanding and nothing more. Someone with a different vantage point,
  today or later, may see a real connection where this iteration saw
  none, the way earlier observers looking at the same horizon concluded
  the earth was flat simply because no one yet had the vantage point to
  see otherwise. Nothing here claims to be the last word on what any of
  this *means*.
- **A specific, checkable computation.** When a note says a formula gives
  64 and not 1 for a stated input, that is not an interpretation that
  wisdom might later overturn — anyone can rerun the same arithmetic
  today, in ten years, or in a hundred, and get the same answer. Calling
  this "just our current understanding" would blur a useful distinction:
  between a vantage point we might not have found yet, and a computation
  that will always come out the same way regardless of vantage point.
  Marking a specific claim as not holding up, as written, is not a
  verdict on the idea behind it — only on that one formulation. The idea
  itself stays open.

So: mistakes stay visible, marked, and open rather than deleted or
declared permanently closed — but marked as what they specifically are,
computational or interpretive, rather than flattening both into the same
level of uncertainty.
